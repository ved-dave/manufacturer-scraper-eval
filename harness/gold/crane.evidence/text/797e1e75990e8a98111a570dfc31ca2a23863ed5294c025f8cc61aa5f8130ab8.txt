The D102B and D103B iron valves;
•	 Consist of a two/three port characterised 
control valve
•	 Provide Class IV leakage during 
isolation function
•	 Are manufactured from Cast Iron, with Brass 
plug and CRNi Steel stem
•	 Offer female threaded connections
•	 Offer double EPDM O-ring stem packing
•	 Have an operating temperature of 5°C to 95°C
•	 Have a maximum operating pressure of 16 bar
•	 Are compatible with Crane Modulating 
ACT993M, 3 Point ACT993TP and Failsafe 
ACT993FS actuators
PRODUCT OVERVIEW
These valves have been categorised in 
accordance with the Pressure Equipment 
Directive–PED. 
The fluid to be transported is limited to group 2 
liquids i.e. non-hazardous. On no account must 
these valves be used on any group 1 liquids, 
group 1 gases or group 2 gases.

MOUNTING INSTRUCTIONS
The valve must be mounted horizontally in all applications where the combined temperature of 
the fluid and the room will be above 50°C, as this is the maximum recommended temperature for 
the actuator to operate normally.
The actuators must not be installed in explosive environments and must not be subjected to steam 
jets or dripping water. 
Leave sufficient room over the actuator, at least 10-15cm, to allow the actuator to be removed from 
the valve body for eventual maintenance.

MOUNTING INSTRUCTIONS ( C ON T I N U E D )
Pipe flushing 
An abnormal valve flow action is caused, in almost all case, by weld slag or foreign bodies entrapped 
between the valve seat and the plug, often resulting in damages. 
To prevent any issues, it is advisable to use strainers upstream of the valve. Moreover, the pipelines 
must be thoroughly washed by positioning the valve stem at half stroke; this operation must be 
performed before start-up and after a prolonged shutdown of the system.
The Crane  D102B & D103B valves are maintenance free but for optimum and long life service the 
following points must be considered: 
Note: The valve should be at zero pressure and ambient temperature prior to any inspection. 
Maintenance Engineers & Operators are reminded to use correct fitting tools and equipment. A full 
risk assessment and methodology statement must be compiled prior to any maintenance.
•	 At minimum, annually, operate valves that remain open or closed for long periods to ensure they 
are in good working order, thus avoiding the possibility of being inoperable in a time of emergency.
•	 The risk assessment must take into account the possibility of the limits of use being exceeded 
whereby a potential hazard could result. 
•	 A maintenance program should, therefore, include checks on the development of unforeseen 
conditions, which could lead to failure.
