Preparation
•	 Ensure metering station is suitable for service 
conditions e.g. pressure, temperature, service 
media. 
•	 All special packaging material should be 
removed prior to installation.
•	 Immediately prior to valve installation, the 
pipework to which the metering station is to 
be fastened should be checked for cleanliness 
and freedom from debris.
•	 Metering stations are precision manufactured 
items and as such, should not be subjected to 
misuse such as careless handling, allowing dirt 
to enter the metering station through the end 
ports, lack of cleaning both metering station 
and system before operation and excessive 
force during assembly.
Threaded Joints
•	 Every effort should be taken to ensure that pipe 
threading is cut to the correct thread form. We 
recommend using a thread gauge to confirm 
correct thread form.
•	 Thread sealing compounds appropriate to the 
application must be used but excessive use 
should be avoided, since this increases thread 
interference and may cause overstressing 
of the body ends. Excessive sealant may 
be extruded into the metering station bore 
affecting the signal readings and flow 
measurement accuracy.
•	 It is important that the internal pipe bore at 
the point upstream to the metering station is 
free from internal burrs, weld spatter or other 
defects which will disrupt the velocity profile of 
the liquid and induce inaccuracies outside the 
flowrate accuracy specified.

Layout and Siting
•	 Valves should be located to ensure ease and 
safety of operation and access allowed for 
subsequent maintenance of the valve.
•	 These metering stations should be installed 
with a minimum of 5 diameters of straight pipe 
upstream having the same nominal diameter 
as the metering station and should not include 
any reducers or any other intrusions into the 
bore within this specified length. 
Please note flow measurement devices are 
designed to give accurate readings when used 
with copper and steel pipework of same size (i.e. 
same DN).
If alternative pipe sizes or materials are used (e.g. 
plastic piping with smaller internal diameter) then 
please contact Crane Technical Department for 
appropriate correction factors.
•	 These metering stations may be installed close 
coupled to a gate valve, ball valve or double 
regulating globe valve.
•	 When the metering stations are used in 
isolation, a minimum of 3 diameters of straight 
pipe must be fitted downstream.
•	 It is important to ensure that the flow arrow 
on the metering station is coincident with 
the direction of flow in the pipeline. If close 
coupled to a valve the metering station must 
be upstream, that is the flow passing through 
the metering station before the valve.
•	 Conventionally, metering stations are installed 
in horizontal pipelines but it is acceptable to 
install in any attitude.
•	 It is preferable to have the plane of the 
test points above horizontal to prevent the 
accumulation of debris.

In horizontal pipework at low level
•	 With the upstream high pressure (red) test

that there is a 100mm clearance to allow a 
manometer probe to be connected to the test 
point.
•	 This may mean that one test point (high 
pressure or low pressure) has to point up at 45 
degrees which will still limit the extent to which 
its opposite serves as a dirt pocket, i.e. keep 
test points away from the bottom of the pipe.
In horizontal pipework at high level
•	 As for low level, where pipework is enclosed 
in ceilings or voids it may be difficult to reach 
across other pipework, cable trays etc. in order 
to connect the manometer probes to the test 
points on the topside of the pipe.
•	 Where this difficulty is likely to occur, it may be 
advantageous to have both test points pointing 
down at 45 degrees. This will ensure that 
neither is located as an obvious dirt pocket.
In vertical pipework
•	 For vertical pipework, there are no obvious dirt 
entrapment areas and therefore the orientation 
of the test points is fully dependent upon the 
ease of manometer probe connection.

LIMITS OF USE
•	 Metering stations are not designed to operate 
under high shock loadings. Where pressure 
increases occur due to shock loading (water 
hammer), they should be added to the working 
pressure to obtain the total pressure acting 
on the metering stations. The total must not 
exceed the pressure rating of the metering 
stations. A pressure surge, or shock, is usually 
caused by the rapid closure of a check valve 
or quarter turn valve resulting in a sudden 
reduction in flow rate.
•	 It is the responsibility of the installer to ensure 
that the metering stations do not exceed 
the allowable limits of pressure. However, 
the equipment is designed to withstand a 
momentary pressure surge of up to 10% above 
the maximum working pressure.
•	 The product has not been designed to include 
corrosion, erosion or abrasion allowances. Any 
•	 Metering stations must be installed into a 
well-designed system and it is recommended 
that the system be inspected in accordance 
with the appropriate national and regional 
legislation.
•	 Metering stations must be installed by 
competent personnel only.
•	 Maximum operating pressure reduces as 
service temperature increases. Service 
temperature and pressure indicated on body 
marking and in the product specification 
should not be exceeded.
•	 The installation should be designed to    
provide adequate means of draining and 
venting to avoid harmful effects such as water 
hammer, vacuum collapse, corrosion and 
uncontrolled chemical reactions and to permit 
cleaning, inspection and maintenance in the 
correct manner.
