Manufacturer product index: what we built, what we learned, what we propose

Team packet · October 2026 · Everything from the rulebook and answer keys through the current scraper, the Approach B experiments and the proposals. Percentages are against the answer keys unless stated.

1. Summary

The goal. Turn manufacturer websites into a clean product index that is about 97% accurate and works for any manufacturer without custom code. The index has one record per product family, holding its documents, CAD files, images, specs, sizes/part numbers and prices.

What we did, in order:

  1. Wrote the rulebook (SPEC v1): what counts as a family, a document, a good image, a spec value or a price.
  2. Built answer keys for 4 manufacturers (Bray, Crane, Cimberio, Sioux Chief) and a scorer, so every version is measured the same way.
  3. Built a new scraper (the "current scraper") and improved it over 10 rounds. It then failed the test on sites it had never seen.
  4. Ran a gap analysis against the old agent's original data. Most misses were items we had seen and then filtered out.
  5. Designed "collect wide, sort smart" and tested it (Approach B). The old agent's link list goes in, and a new sorting step produces the index. We ran 10 experiments on it, including an AI helper.
  6. Built the site-profile step, which reads how each manufacturer groups its own products. On Sioux Chief it cut families from 4,574 to 603 (700 real ones), but a few merged cards still hurt document accuracy (section 10a).

The five things we learned:

  1. Finding links is mostly solved; sorting them is the problem. The old agent finds nearly everything. Every tool loses its points afterwards: deciding what a page is, grouping part numbers into families, choosing files and reading specs.
  2. Fixed rules break on new sites. Rules such as "more than 15 links = category page" or "assets on other domains are junk" worked on the two sites we tuned on, then failed on the next ones.
  3. Every manufacturer already says what its families are, through family pages, category cards or size tables. We should read that structure, not guess it from part numbers.
  4. AI helps only where it's aimed well. Asking it "are these two pages the same family?" was 58% right and couldn't scale. Reading spec tables and settling conflicts, with a verified quote, is the better use.
  5. Tuning on a few sites flatters you. Ten rounds of gains on 2 sites didn't carry over to new ones. Only scoring on unseen manufacturers proves "works for all".

What we propose (section 10):

  1. A "how is this site organised?" step, run on every site. Built; first results in 10a. Next: a shared shape catalogue, so new sites need settings only, not new rules.
  2. A few free extra fields from the old agent, "found on" and page copies first.
  3. Better spec reading, with AI backed by quotes.
  4. Answer keys for 3–5 new manufacturers, to test blind.

Later, the database tables in DATA_MODEL.md.

2. The starting point: the old agent's output

The old agent crawls a manufacturer's site and writes a CSV with two columns: url and url_type. We have its output files only, not its code.

Sioux Chief15,017100%100%100%100%One page per part number (6,700+ pages for 700 families)

"In list" means the item appears anywhere in the CSV. It doesn't mean the agent knows which product the item belongs to.

What it does well: discovery. Its recall is higher than any crawler we built. It also reached Dixon and Apollo, two sites that block our requests completely.

What it lacks:

  • No relationships. It doesn't record which page a link was found on, so we can't tell which product a PDF or image belongs to without crawling again.
  • No sorting. There are no families, part numbers, specs or prices, and a lot of noise: on Bray, 9 in 10 page rows aren't products.
  • Inconsistent labels. The same kind of file gets different url_type labels on different sites, for example CAD viewers labelled as "document".
  • Some discovery gaps. Cimberio's price list and about half its main documents are missing.

3. The rulebook (SPEC v1)

Before measuring anything, we wrote down exactly what a correct answer looks like. It was locked on 9 Oct 2026 and clarified 11 times during the work, each time when two labellers disagreed.

TopicRule in short
Unit of recordOne record per product family, with one canonical URL. A variant is an orderable item with its own part number. Option codes (e.g. ".AT", suffixes) are not variants.
CrawlingPolite: about 1 request per second per host, honest user agent, no bot-protection workarounds. Pages that robots.txt disallows are in scope.
LanguageUS/CA English first, then generic English. International English is kept but flagged. Multilingual docs only count if English comes first and is at least 50% of the text.
DocumentsAll PDFs, labelled by type (spec sheet, submittal, IOM manual, brochure, price list…). The type is decided by content, not file name.
ImagesProduct photos and dimensional drawings only. Keep the largest version (at least 300 px), with no duplicates, infographics or installation/lifestyle photos.
CADDirect files are recorded. Hosted or gated CAD libraries are recorded as links only; no forms are filled in.
Specs13 fields (materials, end connections, pressure, temperature, sizes, standards, certifications, applications…), using controlled vocabularies. Page and spec sheet are cross-checked as agree, refines or conflict. Conflicts are kept, never silently resolved.
EvidenceEvery spec value cites a verbatim quote from a stored copy of its source, and the validator checks the quote.
PriceFrom manufacturer sources only, per variant. Availability is always filled: price shown, login required, request a quote, or not published.

4. Answer keys and the scorer

How each key was built (one labelling session per site, with a checkpoint for the owner's corrections):

  1. A complete family list from the site's sitemap and its category navigation, using a written grouping rule. Family counts: Bray 103, Crane 217, Cimberio 32, Sioux Chief 700.
  2. 15 families per site labelled in depth, picked at random (seed 42): every document, image, CAD file, variant, price and spec, each backed by a stored quote.
  3. An ambiguity log recording every judgement call and its reason.
  4. A blind second labeller re-labelled 6 families per key without seeing the first key. Disagreements were resolved by clarifying the rulebook.
KeyBlind agreement, before → after rulebook fixesMain sources of disagreement
Cranespecs 78% → 95%; documents 20/29 → 29/29; images → 17/17What "standards" covers; image URLs that no page links to
Cimberiospecs 81% → 82%; variants 27/27; images 9/9; CAD 2/2The blind labeller missed a public price list; material wording
Bray (images + doc types)doc types 73% → 100%; images 76% → 88%Led to the "classify by content" and image edge-case rules
Sioux Chiefvocabulary pass onlyIts family grouping was approved by the owner at the checkpoint

Limits to keep in mind:

  • Docs, images, specs and prices are measured on 15 families per site. Family counts use the full list.
  • Sioux Chief's 700 is the key's catalog model: one family per category card, split where a card mixes product lines (28 times). Spare-parts lines count as separate families. Only the 15 deep families were checked one by one.
  • Apollo and Dixon block automated access, so they have no key. Milwaukee and Caleffi are kept untouched as a future blind test and have no keys yet.

The scorer checks every output file against the rulebook: schema, quotes, wrong-language and duplicate checks. It then scores each measure against the key: families found and correct, duplicates, docs, CAD, images (including best version), variants, specs (accuracy and completeness, by field), conflicts, prices. It can also score the old agent's CSV, but only for "is it in the list?". It never fetches anything, so scores are repeatable.

Why it failed:

  1. Sioux Chief has one page per part number. The scraper treated each page as a separate family (6,270 vs 700), which scattered docs, images and sizes across them.
  2. Cimberio stores its images and most datasheets on its store/CDN domains. The scraper refused to follow those links.
  3. Shared datasheets were discarded as "site chrome", even when their text named the product.
  4. The image filter was too strict (about 79% found even on the development sites).
  5. It was slow: fetching one file at a time and parsing the same PDFs again and again.

We fixed the speed part afterwards. Each PDF is now parsed once, keyed by its content fingerprint: on Bray a run went from 13 min to 7 min, and to 1 min 40 s on a re-run, with byte-identical output.

7. The new design: "collect wide, sort smart"

The old agent collects too much and doesn't sort it. The current scraper sorts well but filters too hard. The new design combines their strengths:

LayerJobWho
1. CollectEvery page, PDF, image and CAD link, on any host (CDNs included), with where each was found.The old agent (decided). Today it supplies bare CSVs, later the extra fields in section 10b.
2. Sort with rulesGroup pages into families; drop obvious junk; keep obvious matches; read easy fields. Every item gets a keep / drop / unsure decision with a reason, and nothing is dropped silently.The new processing step (Approach B), deterministic
3. AI for unsure items only"Does this file belong to product X?", free-text specs, page-vs-PDF conflicts. The AI must quote the source text exactly, and the quote is checked.Resolver, with cached answers and a budget limit
Output + auditOne record per family in the existing format, plus a list of everything rejected and why.Scored by the same harness

Self-healing loop (designed, not yet tested). The AI answers unsure items. When it answers many similar items the same way, the pattern becomes a reusable data rule: a spec-name synonym, a section rule or a per-site profile entry. Cases a data rule can't express go to a coding agent as a reviewed PR. Over time there are fewer unsure items and AI cost falls.

Guardrails:

  • Answer keys only measure, never teach.
  • No quote, no answer.
  • Every rule is versioned and can be rolled back.
  • A new rule can't silently change a confident decision.
  • Held-out sites stay untouched.
  • Cost is bounded per site.

8. Approach B: what we built and what each experiment showed

Built:

  • A process command that takes the old agent's link file and writes the index in the same format as the current scraper.
  • A decisions log: every keep, drop or unsure decision, with its reason and evidence.
  • Blocked-site tracking: after 8 blocked replies in a row, it stops asking that host.
  • Fetching that works on several hosts at once while staying polite to each.
  • A reader for today's CSV (L0) and for the richer format we propose (L1), with a validator.
  • An L1 simulator, and a resolver (AI helper) with quote checking, caching and a budget.

Rule for every experiment (the keep rule): the average score must rise, and no key measure on any site may drop more than 2 points.

The AI helper test, in detail

We used Claude (Sonnet, through a subscription login) on Sioux Chief's 497 "same family?" questions. It answered 323 before the usage limit, and we stopped there.

  • Verified "different": 256, of which 83% were right. Verified "same": 59, of which 58% were right. Mostly it merged spare-parts pages with the product they serve. The quotes were real, so the quote check couldn't catch that kind of judgement error.
  • Why it can't close the gap: each answer merges at most two families, and most duplicate families never become a question at all. A perfect run would still leave about 4,100 families against 700.
  • Cost: free on the subscription; about $13 for 497 questions at API prices.

10. Proposals

10a. Site profile: "how is this site organised?" (built, first results measured)

What it is. A new first step in processing. It runs after the old agent and before grouping: old agent collects → site profile → grouping and sorting → scoring. It needs nothing new from the old agent: it reads the existing link list and saved page copies, with no new crawling.

How it works. It groups the links by URL pattern and opens 5 pages of each pattern. It looks for a repeated "card" layout (many small boxes, each with a name, a link and a part number). Then it tests five family shapes on about 50 sampled product pages:

  • Category cards: each card on a category page is a family.
  • Family pages: one page lists several part numbers.
  • Item tables: a page lists its sibling part numbers.
  • Shared spec sheet: a hint only, because one sheet can cover several families.
  • One page per model.

Each shape is scored on how many pages it explains and how often it contradicts itself. The winner is written to a short readable file, <site>.profile.json, with the reason and example pages. A person can check or correct it by hand. It takes 3–15 seconds per site. The answer keys are never used to build it.

SiteShape pickedEvidence from the sample
Sioux ChiefCategory cardsCards name the family of 100% of sampled pages, with no contradictions. 164 category pages read (out of about 7,000 pages); 698 card families.
BrayFamily pages88% of sampled pages list several part numbers
CraneOne page per model95% of sampled pages are one model each
CimberioOne page per model91% of sampled pages are one model each

Grouping follows the profile. Pages the profile covers go into its families first. Today's rules only handle the rest, and they can never merge two families the profile keeps apart. On Bray, Crane and Cimberio, today's rules already matched the site's shape, so their scores are exactly unchanged.

  • The fix between versions: parent category pages repeat their sub-categories' cards under the same names, and the first version merged them.   • What still fails: on a few cards, the site lists several product lines under one name, which the answer key splits by hand. For example, one card covers 6 families in the key. A merged family picks up the other families' documents and images. Two of the 15 hand-checked families are like this, so "correct" main docs fall from 92% to 46%. By our keep rule (no key number may drop more than 2 points), it doesn't pass yet. So the profile is opt-in (process --profile), not the default.
  • Why some profile families are still mixed:
    • One card shows the same product across several pipe types, while the key splits by pipe type.
    • The card names a size or connection; the real family name (the series) sits above the card.
    • A small card includes related items, such as a tool or accessory, that the key treats as their own family.

Proposed next: a shared shape catalogue (written, not yet built or tested)

The goal is a profile step that is repeatable and accurate on new manufacturers, with no rules written for a single site.

  1. Rules live in one harness document (SITE_PROFILE.md, next to SPEC.md). It lists a small, fixed set of general family shapes: category cards, family pages, item tables, one page per model. It also lists building blocks, such as "series heading above the cards" or "pipe type in the category or product name". For each one it states how to detect it, its pass bar (coverage and contradictions), how families are formed, and its unit tests.
  2. A site profile holds settings only, never logic. For example: shape = category cards; card = this box; family name = series heading + card name. The same code runs for every site.
  3. Code tries first. If a catalogue shape passes its bar, code builds the profile in seconds, for $0, with no AI.
  4. New, unrecognised sites go to the site profile agent (below), which may only choose from the catalogue.
  5. The catalogue changes only by a reviewed PR. A new shape must be described in general terms, work on at least 2 manufacturers (including one it wasn't built on), and make no existing site worse (keep rule).
  6. Proof is blind. We build answer keys for 3–5 new manufacturers, run with no tuning, and report the scores as they are.

Site profile agent (design only, not built)

Code handles every site it recognises. The agent is only for sites where no catalogue shape passes its bar.

QuestionDesign
When it runsOnly when code finds no catalogue shape that passes, or the winning shape has too many contradictions. Off by default.
What it seesThe catalogue (SITE_PROFILE.md), 10–20 sample pages from the old agent's list (saved copies), and code's score for each shape. Never the answer key.
What it returnsA shape and its settings, chosen from the catalogue. For example, which page element is a card and where the family name sits. Each setting is backed by a word-for-word quote from a sample page.
What it may not doInvent a rule, write code, or list families by hand.
How its answer is checkedCode confirms the shape is in the catalogue and every quote appears in the pages. It then rebuilds the families with those settings and scores them on coverage and contradictions.
Pass: the profile is saved, marked "decided by agent".
Fail: it's marked "needs review" and the standard rules are used.
If nothing in the catalogue fitsThe agent says so and describes the pattern. A person decides whether it becomes a new general shape: a reviewed PR that works on 2+ manufacturers and passes the keep rule.
RepeatabilityAccepted settings are saved in the profile. Later runs reuse them with no new AI call, so the same input gives the same output.
CostA few AI calls per new, unrecognised site (estimate); $0 for recognised sites.

10b. Free extra fields from the old agent

The rule is no new requests: the agent records only what it already has in hand while crawling. Output is one JSON line per page or link (the format and validator already exist).

PriorityFieldsWhat it unlocks
1. Relationshipsfound_on (page the link was on), link text, section heading, in header/footer/menu, link kindWhich docs and images belong to which product, without crawling again

Also: one fixed label set for url_type, normalised URLs, and "tag, don't drop": language copies, footer links and off-site links are kept with tags. Acceptance checks: every link's "found on" page exists in the file; run time stays within ±5%; the file passes the validator. Separately, the agent's discovery gaps (Cimberio's price list and documents) need fixing on its side, because no extra field fixes them.

10c. Better specs (the biggest gap on every site)

  • A shared dictionary of spec names, e.g. "Working Press." means pressure rating.
  • Fewer false page-vs-PDF conflicts. Most flagged conflicts are formatting differences, e.g. "-10 to 100" vs "10 - 120°C".
  • AI reads spec tables and PDFs, and every value must come with a verified quote. This is where AI is most useful: an estimated +2–3 average points from conflicts alone.

Effort: about 1 week.

10d. Smaller rule fixes already identified (no new data needed)

  • Match "non-product" URL words only as whole path segments. This rescues Bray's "series-70-hazardous-location", for example.
  • Let spec tables and spec downloads outweigh "listing page" cues for pages without a product word in the URL. This would cover Bray's 2 remaining dropped families.
  • Name combined product photos correctly (Bray images).
  • Keep multi-product documents without creating false spec conflicts (Crane other docs: 55% vs 71%).
  • Keep fingerprinting images the step decides to keep, even when their size is already known.

10e. Prove it on new manufacturers

Build answer keys for 3–5 varied manufacturers, about one session each, using the same process. Include electrical, plumbing and mechanical makers, one with assets on a CDN and one with part-number pages. Then run blind, with no tuning. Milwaukee and Caleffi stay reserved for the final blind test. This is the only honest way to say "it works for all manufacturers".

10f. Database for production (DATA_MODEL.md, design only)

Three services share Postgres tables and never call each other:

  • a link collector (the old agent);
  • a processor (this step);
  • a health checker, which re-checks links and queues the families that changed.
TablesWhy
sites, links, page_links (which page links to which), link_versions (each stored copy with its fingerprint)The relationships are the main grouping evidence. Every value traces back to the exact copy it came from.
families, family_assets (with keep/drop/unsure + reason), variants, spec_values + spec_sources, pricesThe output plus its audit trail. Page and PDF values are both kept; prices sit on the variant, or on the family when it has no variants.
runs; plus ai_decisions and rules for the self-healing loopRuns can be compared and rolled back. Only new or changed links are reprocessed, which is essential at thousands of sites.

Evaluation stays file-based, through an exporter, so the answer keys keep working. Migration: loader → storage interface → old agent writes to the tables → schedule the services.

Honest status against 97%: we are not there. Families on page-per-family sites (Bray, Crane, Cimberio) are at 93–100%. On Sioux Chief, the site profile brings families to 603 (700 real), but main-document accuracy is still too low. Specs (15–67%) are far off everywhere. Steps 1–2 target grouping and documents, step 3 targets specs, and step 4 is the proof.

Decisions the team needs to make:

  1. Can we get the old agent's code, or ask its owners for the extra fields?
  2. For the AI layer: which model, what cost per site is acceptable, and should learned rules apply automatically or go to review first?
  3. Is some run-to-run variation acceptable on the unsure items? It can be reduced with fixed model versions and cached answers.
  4. What is the speed target for a 6,000-product catalogue?
  5. Which manufacturers get answer keys next?
  6. Should spare-parts lines count as separate families (as the keys do now) or join their product?
  7. Who reviews and approves new shapes in the catalogue, and is a person needed to confirm each new site's profile at first?
  8. When a site's own category card covers several product lines, is that one family (as the site shows it) or several (as the key splits it)?

Risks:

  • Collecting wider means more for the sorter to get wrong. This is why there's a precision gate.
  • AI answers can be confidently wrong, even with real quotes, as the 58% result showed. Use AI for reading, not for structural decisions.
  • Blocked sites can't be scored.
  • Results on only a few sites misled us once already.
